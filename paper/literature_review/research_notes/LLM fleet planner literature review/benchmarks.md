# Benchmarks for LLM/VLM embodied and multi-robot planning (2023-2026)

Scope note: about 20 search and fetch calls. Most facts come from search-result
summaries of arXiv, proceedings, or project pages; items marked **[fetched]**
were read in full on the primary page. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** come from
background knowledge and no source was retrieved in this session. Check them
before citing.

## Q1. Catalog: domain, single vs multi-robot, simulator, scoring, size, classical baselines, reproducibility, venue

### Takeaway
The household-embodied benchmarks (PARTNR, EmbodiedBench, LoTa-Bench, Habitat
3.0, BEHAVIOR-1K, TDW-MAT/C-WAH, COHERENT, SMART-LLM) are the closest in
spirit. None of them combines an aerial heterogeneous fleet, a scripted event
stream, exactly replayable episodes, and a leaderboard that puts LLMs beside
classical optimizers and auctions. The aerial benchmarks are almost all
single-UAV vision-language navigation. The one multi-UAV LLM benchmark found
(MultiUAV-Plat, 2026) has no classical baseline and no heterogeneity.

### Cited Findings

**Household / indoor embodied (multi-agent)**
- **PARTNR** (Meta FAIR; arXiv 2411.00081, ICLR 2025). Human-robot
  coordination in household tasks in Habitat simulation. The 100,000
  natural-language instructions come with tailored evaluation functions and
  fall into four types: constraint-free, spatial, temporal, and heterogeneous
  (one agent cannot do some actions). It spans 60 houses and 5,819 objects.
  Tasks were generated by an LLM with simulation-in-the-loop verification. —
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00081v1);
  [ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/a3cf318fbeec1126da21e9185ae9908c-Abstract-Conference.html);
  [project](https://aihabitat.org/partnr/)
  - Results: humans solve 93% of tasks and LLMs only 30%. The planners
    coordinate poorly and fail at task tracking and error recovery. Smaller
    fine-tuned models matched models 9x larger and ran 8.6x faster at
    inference. (The 93/30 figures were seen only on the project page.) —
    [project](https://aihabitat.org/partnr/)
- **Habitat 3.0** (Meta; arXiv 2310.13724, ICLR 2024). A simulator with
  humanoid avatars and human-in-the-loop (mouse, keyboard, VR). Its two tasks
  are Social Navigation (find and follow a humanoid) and Social Rearrangement
  (robot and humanoid rearrange objects together). Its baselines are learned
  policies, not LLMs. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.13724);
  [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/19442)
- **TDW-MAT and C-WAH, used by CoELA** (arXiv 2307.02485, ICLR 2024).
  TDW-MAT is a ThreeDWorld multi-agent transport task with physics, "food" and
  "stuff" goods, and Enough-Container vs. Rare-Container settings, scored by
  transport rate and efficiency. C-WAH is Communicative Watch-And-Help on
  VirtualHome. CoELA driven by GPT-4 "can surpass strong planning-based
  methods". The comparison included planning baselines, an MCTS-based
  hierarchical planner as I recall [UNVERIFIED detail]. —
  [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/19093);
  [code](https://github.com/UMass-Foundation-Model/Co-LLM-Agents)
  - Follow-up CaPo (arXiv 2411.04679) found that CoELA's single-step planning
    produces "extemporaneous and incoherent actions among agents". —
    [CaPo](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04679v2)
- **COHERENT** (Liu, Tang et al.; arXiv 2409.15146, ICRA 2025). An LLM
  planner for a heterogeneous team (quadrotors, robot dogs, robot arms) with a
  proposal, execution, feedback, and adjustment loop. Its benchmark has 5
  large scenes and 100 long-horizon tasks, and it was also tried on real
  robots. A 2025 survey says the benchmark is built on BEHAVIOR-1K
  (secondhand, not confirmed in the original). It is one of very few LLM
  benchmarks with an aerial agent in a heterogeneous team. —
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15146v3);
  [survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.03814)
- **SMART-LLM** (arXiv 2309.10062; IROS 2024 [UNVERIFIED venue]). Multi-robot
  planning in AI2-THOR. The LLM does task decomposition, coalition formation,
  and task allocation through few-shot programmatic prompts. The benchmark has
  four categories of high-level instructions of varying complexity, and test
  floorplans are unseen. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.10062v2)
- **LoTa-Bench** (Choi et al.; arXiv 2402.08178, ICLR 2024). Single-agent and
  automatic: it scores home-service task planners on ALFRED/AI2-THOR and on an
  extension of Watch-And-Help/VirtualHome. —
  [ICLR](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/e91fb65c6324a984ea9ef39a5b84af04-Abstract-Conference.html);
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08178v1)
- **BEHAVIOR-1K** (CoRL 2022 / PMLR v205; later arXiv revisions). 1,000
  household activities in 50 scenes on OmniGibson. Sources disagree on the
  object count: the CoRL paper says more than 5,000 and later arXiv says more
  than 9,000. It predates 2023 but is the substrate for later LLM work. —
  [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v205/li23a.html);
  [site](https://behavior.stanford.edu)
- **ALFRED / ALFWorld** (pre-2023: CVPR 2020 / ICLR 2021 [UNVERIFIED
  venues]). ALFWorld is TextWorld built on ALFRED tasks. One paper reports
  3,554 task instances. — [NeSyPr citing it](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.19429)
- **RoboCasa** (arXiv 2406.02523, RSS 2024). Kitchen manipulation: 100 tasks
  (25 atomic, 75 composite and LLM-suggested), 120 scenes, 2,509 objects, and
  more than 100k MimicGen trajectories. It benchmarks policy learning, not LLM
  planners. RoboCasa365 (ICLR 2026) extends it to 365 tasks. —
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.02523);
  [RoboCasa365](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04356)

**MLLM / agent suites**
- **EmbodiedBench** (Yang, Zhang et al.; arXiv 2502.09560, ICML 2025 oral).
  1,128 tasks in 4 environments, from high-level household tasks to low-level
  navigation and manipulation, plus 6 capability subsets (commonsense,
  spatial, long-horizon, and others). 24 models were tested, and the best,
  GPT-4o, averaged 28.9%. Models are fine at high level and weak at low-level
  manipulation. Single agent. — [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2502.09560);
  [ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/oral/47252)
- **AgentBench** (arXiv 2308.03688, ICLR 2024). 8 environments: OS, DB,
  knowledge graph, card game, lateral thinking, house-holding (ALFWorld), web
  shopping, and web browsing. 29 LLMs were tested, with a large gap between
  commercial and ≤70B open models. —
  [ICLR](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/e9df36b21ff4ee211a8b71ee8b7e9f57-Abstract-Conference.html);
  [GitHub](https://github.com/THUDM/AgentBench)
- **VisualAgentBench** (ICLR 2025). 5 environments: OmniGibson, Minecraft,
  Mobile, WebArena-Lite, and CSS. 18 LMMs were tested, and it uses
  program-based solvers to make trajectory data. —
  [ICLR 2025 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/eea71dc576381b88f2a0ca4dedc2140d-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **MultiAgentBench / MARBLE** (Zhu et al., UIUC; arXiv 2503.01935, ACL 2025,
  pp. 8580-8622). Text-only multi-agent collaboration and competition, scored
  by milestone-based KPIs. It compares star, chain, tree, and graph
  topologies. The graph topology was best in research scenarios, cognitive
  planning raised milestones by 3%, and gpt-4o-mini had the highest average
  task score. The code repository is listed at two different GitHub URLs. —
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01935v1);
  [ACL](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.acl-long.421)
- **RoboBench** (arXiv 2510.17801). Evaluates MLLMs as an "embodied brain". —
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17801)

**Aerial**
- **MultiUAV-Plat** (Zhang et al., NUDT; arXiv 2606.31073, 2026 preprint)
  **[fetched]**. An LLM-oriented multi-UAV platform with RESTful APIs,
  role-based information access, local perception only, and hidden
  validators. Its 75 mission sessions (3-30 UAVs each), 1,500
  natural-language tasks, and 9,396 hidden checks cover three families: Area
  Search, Area Assignment & Patrol, and Target Assignment, at 5 difficulty
  levels. Metrics are Task Pass Rate, Average and Global Check Pass Rate, and
  Failed Task Rate. **No classical baselines**: it compares only ReAct with
  its own Agent4Drone. Best result: Agent4Drone with deepseek-v4-pro at 70.27%
  task pass; ReAct with doubao-2-pro was lowest at 30.60%. **No heterogeneous
  UAV capabilities** are described. Sessions are seeded, but determinism of
  LLM outputs is not discussed. The obstacles are static, and richer dynamics
  are left to future work. Code: GitHub zhangsheng93/MultiUAV-Plat. —
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31073v1)
- **UAV-ON** (HIT Shenzhen; arXiv 2508.00288). Open-world object-goal
  navigation for a single aerial agent: 14 Unreal Engine environments and
  1,270 target objects. It has a zero-shot MLLM baseline, and "all baselines
  struggle". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00288v4)
- **OpenFly** (arXiv 2502.18041). An aerial VLN toolchain and benchmark with
  100k trajectories in 18 scenes, rendered with UE, GTA V, Google Earth, and
  3DGS. It includes the OpenFly-Agent model. Single UAV. —
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18041v6)
- **AerialVLN** (ICCV 2023 [UNVERIFIED venue]), **AVDN** (about 3k drone
  trajectories with human dialogs), **CityNav**, and **OpenUAV**: a 2026
  survey calls these the mature aerial VLN benchmarks and reports that
  LLM/VLM methods improve NE and SPL. All are single-UAV navigation. —
  [survey arXiv 2604.07705](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.07705)
- **UAV-Flow**: described as the first real-world benchmark for
  language-conditioned fine-grained UAV control, short horizon. —
  [AAAI listing](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/39562/43523)
  (attribution taken from a search summary; verify)
- **UAVBench** (arXiv 2511.11252). 50,000 UAV flight scenarios generated by an
  LLM and safety-validated, plus multiple-choice reasoning sets that include
  multi-agent coordination. A dataset, not a closed-loop benchmark. —
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11252);
  [GitHub](https://github.com/maferrag/UAVBench)
- Seen by title only, not read: **α³-Bench** (safety, robustness, and
  efficiency of LLM UAV agents over 6G; arXiv 2601.03281), **ESARBench**
  (agentic UAV search and rescue; arXiv 2605.01371), and **"Say the Mission,
  Execute the Swarm"** (LLM function-calling swarm management, WoWMoM 2026;
  arXiv 2605.03788). —
  [α³](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.03281);
  [ESAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.01371);
  [WoD](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.03788v1)

**Driving**
- **DriveLM** (arXiv 2312.14150, ECCV 2024 oral). Graph VQA over perception,
  prediction, and planning, on nuScenes and CARLA. Open-loop QA. —
  [ECCV](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/html/6870_ECCV_2024_paper.php)
- **LMDrive / LangAuto** (arXiv 2312.07488, CVPR 2024). Closed-loop
  language-guided driving in CARLA with about 64k instruction clips. The
  LangAuto benchmark includes misleading and long instructions and
  adversarial scenarios. —
  [CVPR](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Shao_LMDrive_Closed-Loop_End-to-End_Driving_with_Large_Language_Models_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf)

**Routing / dispatch / allocation (LLM vs OR)**
- **RoutBench / ARS** (arXiv 2502.15359). 1,000 VRP variants from 24
  attributes. The LLM agent writes constraint code for a solver. It reaches
  above 90% success on common VRPs and above 60% on RoutBench, and is compared
  with 7 LLM methods and common solvers. Here the LLM is a modeler, not a
  solver. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15359v3)
- **MRTA-Benchmark dataset** (4TU.ResearchData, 2025). 250,000 heterogeneous
  MRTA instances with precedence and coalitions, each solved optimally by an
  exact MILP. A ready source of ground-truth optima. —
  [4TU](https://data.4tu.nl/datasets/10e28ee0-9ad9-450d-8be7-6e6a91f2931f)
- **Automatic MILP construction for MRTA** (arXiv 2503.13813, IROS 2025).
  Formulation accuracy with a knowledge base: Qwen2.5-7B 28%, Llama-3-8B 32%,
  DeepSeek-R1-Distill-32B 82%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13813)
- **FLEET** (arXiv 2510.07417). LLM front-end with a MILP or auction back-end
  for heterogeneous teams. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07417)
- **RobotFleet** (Gupta, Asbery, Merchant, Anwar, Thomason; arXiv
  2510.10379). An open-source centralized multi-robot planning framework that
  reportedly compares an LLM allocator with a min-max-load MILP under
  capability constraints. Its numbers could not be extracted. —
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.10379)
- **LTAA construction benchmark** (Kaitha & Yu; arXiv 2512.02810)
  **[fetched abstract]**. An LLM allocator vs. DP, Q-learning, and DQN on
  TEACh-derived construction tasks, with SMART-LLM replicated. —
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02810v1)
- **CoMuRoS** (arXiv 2511.22354; Frontiers in Robotics & AI 2026).
  Heterogeneous teams with event-driven LLM replanning; GPT-4o succeeded in 9
  of 10 hardware trials. No solver baseline. —
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22354v2);
  [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13482247/)

### Inferences
- **Reproducibility.** Simulator-based benchmarks (Habitat, AI2-THOR, TDW,
  OmniGibson) are seeded, but none claimed byte-identical replay, and the
  sources found say little about it. MultiUAV-Plat seeds its sessions but
  carries final UAV positions between tasks, so results depend on the order
  of tasks. Exact replay is an open niche to claim, carefully.
- **Coverage.** Heterogeneity appears in PARTNR (as a task type), COHERENT,
  FLEET, and CoMuRoS. Aerial agents appear in COHERENT (quadrotors in a mixed
  team) and in MultiUAV-Plat (homogeneous UAVs). No benchmark found has a
  heterogeneous *aerial* fleet.
- **Events.** Scripted dynamic event streams are essentially absent.
  CoMuRoS has event-driven replanning on hardware, but not as a benchmark
  with a fixed event script.

### Gaps
- No primary source retrieved for: ManiSkill LLM tasks, an "MRBench"
  multi-robot LLM benchmark (none found under that name), the CityNav and
  AerialVLN original papers, SMART-LLM's venue and success numbers,
  LoTa-Bench numbers, or adoption counts (citations, leaderboard
  submissions) for any benchmark.
- No dedicated "LLM dispatch" benchmark was found apart from RoutBench and
  the LLM-as-OR-modeler line.

## Q2. Which include classical planners/optimizers as baselines, and what did they find?

### Takeaway
When the problem is fully specified (PDDL, VRP, MRTA with known
capabilities), classical solvers dominate LLMs on correctness and speed. LLMs
are competitive mainly as *modelers* that hand a formulation to a solver, or
in loosely specified, partially observed, communication-heavy embodied tasks.
In the last case, CoELA claims to beat planning baselines, but that tests
open-ended household cooperation rather than a structured optimization.

### Cited Findings
- On PlanBench, Fast Downward "solves all given instances near-instantly with
  guaranteed perfect accuracy", while LLMs struggle even on the smallest
  instances. o1-preview and o1-mini were notably better than earlier LLMs but
  not perfect (Valmeekam, Stechly, Kambhampati, 2024). —
  [arXiv 2409.13373](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13373v1)
- PlanBench itself is a NeurIPS 2023 Datasets & Benchmarks paper. —
  [NeurIPS D&B](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/7a92bcdede88c7afd108072faf5485c8-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf)
- Goebel & Zips (2025) give LLMs raw PDDL and compare them with Fast Downward
  (seq-sat-lama-2011, 600 s cap). Classical planners are framed as the tool
  for provably correct plans when the domain is precisely modeled. —
  [arXiv 2507.23589](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23589v1)
- In FLEET, an auction allocator matched MILP on constraint-free tasks but
  fell below it on heterogeneous and temporal tasks. This is useful for
  positioning auction vs. optimizer baselines. —
  [arXiv 2510.07417](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07417)
- In ARS/RoutBench, the LLM writes solver code rather than routes, and gets
  above 90% on common VRPs and above 60% on 1,000 variants. —
  [arXiv 2502.15359](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15359v3)
- In MILP construction for MRTA, only the large reasoning model wrote correct
  formulations 82% of the time; small models managed about 30%. —
  [arXiv 2503.13813](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13813)
- In LTAA, an LLM allocator reached 77% completion in a "Heavy Excels"
  specialization setting and is claimed to beat DP, Q-learning, and DQN. The
  baselines' numbers were not visible in the abstract. Baseline SMART-LLM
  scored 0.10 on compound tasks (search snippet). —
  [arXiv 2512.02810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02810v1)
- CoELA (GPT-4) "can surpass strong planning-based methods" on TDW-MAT and
  C-WAH. — [ICLR 2024](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/19093)
- A 2026 survey of LLMs for routing reports comparative experiments of LLM
  methods vs. traditional and neural CO solvers. Its numbers were not
  retrieved. — [arXiv 2607.00604](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2607.00604)

### Inferences
- Most embodied LLM benchmarks (PARTNR, EmbodiedBench, MultiUAV-Plat,
  COHERENT, MARBLE) report only LLM-vs-LLM or LLM-vs-human comparisons. A
  leaderboard that puts LLMs on the same episodes as an optimizer and an
  auction is rare. That is a defensible gap, consistent with FLEET's
  auction-vs-MILP result and PlanBench's Fast Downward result.
- I found no head-to-head of an LLM allocator vs. an auction (e.g. CBBA)
  under injected dynamic events.

### Gaps
- RobotFleet's LLM-vs-MILP numbers (PDF unreadable via fetch). The size of
  the gaps in LTAA's baselines. Whether the CoELA baselines are MCTS or rule
  planners [UNVERIFIED].

## Q3. Aerial, heterogeneous, dynamic event-driven, and capability-reasoning settings

### Takeaway
Capability reasoning is tested explicitly by PARTNR's "heterogeneous" tasks
(one agent cannot do some actions), by COHERENT (quadrotor, dog, and arm
capabilities), and by FLEET and MRTA-Benchmark (capability constraints).
Aerial benchmarks are dominated by single-UAV VLN. Dynamic event streams are
nearly unbenchmarked.

### Cited Findings
- PARTNR's heterogeneous task type: one agent cannot perform some actions. —
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00081v1)
- COHERENT: quadrotors, robotic dogs, and robotic arms; 100 tasks. —
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15146v3)
- MultiUAV-Plat: 3-30 UAVs with no capability heterogeneity described. The
  platform supports dynamic targets, wind, and weather, but the benchmark's
  obstacles are static. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31073v1)
- CoMuRoS: event-driven replanning by an LLM task manager for heterogeneous
  teams. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22354v2)
- MRTA-Benchmark: heterogeneous robots, precedence, coalition formation. —
  [4TU](https://data.4tu.nl/datasets/10e28ee0-9ad9-450d-8be7-6e6a91f2931f)
- Aerial VLN benchmarks (AerialVLN-S, AVDN, OpenUAV, CityNav, OpenFly,
  UAV-ON) are single-agent navigation. —
  [survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.07705);
  [UAV-ON](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00288v4)

### Inferences
- A heterogeneous aerial fleet with scripted events and replayable episodes
  fills a cell no catalogued benchmark occupies. MultiUAV-Plat (2026) is the
  nearest neighbor and should be cited and contrasted: it has no classical
  baseline, homogeneous UAVs, static obstacles, and task-order carry-over.

### Gaps
- Not read beyond titles: α³-Bench, ESARBench, and the Web-of-Drones paper.
  Any of them could include dynamic events or baselines.

## Q4. Common criticisms

### Takeaway
The main criticisms: planning evaluated as multiple choice or by text
similarity rather than executed; heavy reliance on simulation; benchmark
saturation; commonsense tasks that mix retrieval with planning; low absolute
success hiding coordination failures; and LLM-generated tasks.

### Cited Findings
- Existing embodied benchmarks reduce planning to multiple choice or to BLEU
  and generic LLM scoring, miss skill-object dependencies, execution-order
  flexibility, and feasibility, and depend heavily on simulation (RoboBench's
  own framing). — [arXiv 2510.17801](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17801)
- PlanBench's motivation: on common-sense tasks it is hard to tell planning
  from retrieval of training knowledge, and progress since GPT-3 has been
  "surprisingly slow". — [arXiv 2409.13373](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13373v1)
- Benchmark saturation: in a study of 60 LLM benchmarks, nearly half were
  saturated, more so as they age. Private test sets did not help, while
  expert-curated sets resisted saturation better (ICML 2026). —
  [arXiv 2602.16763](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16763)
- PARTNR's LLM planners show poor coordination and fail at task tracking and
  error recovery, and its tasks are themselves LLM-generated. —
  [project](https://aihabitat.org/partnr/)
- CoELA's step-by-step planning yields incoherent multi-agent actions. —
  [CaPo](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04679v2)
- EmbodiedBench: strong high-level scores hide weak low-level control (best
  28.9% average). — [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2502.09560)

### Inferences
- A benchmark that executes plans in a deterministic simulator, scores them
  with physical metrics, uses fixed hand-scripted event streams, and anchors
  results against optimizer and auction baselines answers several of these
  criticisms directly: execution over text matching, retrieval vs. planning,
  and a known optimum or reference instead of an LLM judge.

### Gaps
- No study found that measures run-to-run variance or prompt sensitivity of
  LLM planners in embodied simulators, or contamination in simulator
  benchmarks.
